# Review of the codebase, 8 September 2026

Tracker item: MORF-265. Ten read-only sweeps read the code as it stood at commit 5b102b6 on
the afternoon of 8 September: two on the roller and the town routines, one each on
duplication, refactoring shapes, fragile code, performance and dead code, and one fact-check
per Tidbits page. This report consolidates them. Every claim below was re-checked against
main at commit 3ea0d45, which is thirty commits later and includes the wall colours (MORF-263),
the town building screens (MORF-213), the HIT ANY KEY plaque (MORF-220) and the section screen
(MORF-222). Findings the newer commits had already fixed are noted as such. Nothing under
`src/` was changed by the review; this file is the only addition.

Each finding gives the file and line, why it matters, the fix, and the tracker key it was
filed under. Twenty-three items were filed, all children of MORF-265. Where a finding is
already tracked, the existing key is given instead.

## Verdict

The code is in good shape. The three ports are faithful, well cited, and covered by 210 test
files and about 3,500 tests, all green. The type escapes are gone: no `any`, no ts-ignore, and
every cast on parsed data is followed by a field-by-field check. The random number paths, the
key queue and the Revenge clock are deterministic, so a run log replays. Browser storage goes
through one guarded wrapper. There is no `{@html}` anywhere. The reference bundles are byte
for byte what the game folders hold.

What matters most is small and concrete. Four real bugs: a Revenge run edited mid-play is
logged as unedited and then verified anyway (MORF-268); the Revenge map reader rejects the
site's own export after a Scroll of Seeing and the save editor then imports it as a character
(MORF-269); an error inside a play loop freezes the tab and crashes the verifier with no
verdict (MORF-270); and Revenge's `@` and `#` keys change numbers nothing reads (MORF-271). The
tidbits fact-check found 28 claims the decompiled code contradicts and 22 that mislead, spread
over the three pages (MORF-283, 284, 285). Three of the Revenge ones turned out to be errors in
the port as well (MORF-272, 273, 274). On the performance side, every keypress on the game
screen rebuilds a whole 1024 by 768 frame, and hidden tabs keep animating (MORF-277, 278).

What should be left alone: the ported routines. The per-key handlers, the town menus and the
combat code share almost nothing between games, and where they look alike the decompilation
shows the same routine with the money model swapped and the affordability test flipped. Merging
them would silently change one game. The save editor is already fully data-driven. The
localStorage layer is done right. The bigger refactors below are real, but they are about the
site's own plumbing around the ports, not the ports.

## Refactoring opportunities

The rule the refactoring sweep applied: the same thing built two or three times where one
shape would do, and the site's own precedent for the shape. `SpellBook.svelte` and
`MonsterDatabase.svelte` are shells that take props and snippets, and both games mount them.
That is the pattern for everything below.

**One Play tab shell for the three games.** `src/lib/play/Play.svelte`, `mw/MwPlay.svelte`,
`rev/RevPlay.svelte`. About 265 lines are identical between DotU and MW and about 190 with
Revenge: the state, `start()` and `leave()`, the record-edited effect, the map-follows effect,
the reopen effect, `isTyping`, the landing page, the over-box, the run block, the mode radios,
and 200 lines of CSS that differ in twelve. They have already drifted: the breakpoint is 900 in
one and 1000 in another. A `PlayTab` shell with snippets for the screen, the place line, the
side extras and the panel, plus a `PLAY_GAMES` table beside `RUN_GAMES`. Doing this also fixes
a small inconsistency: `run.ts:591` reports Revenge's replay place with 1-based column and
row, while `RevPlay.svelte:161, 176, 255` subtract one. Harmless today, a trap for the
leaderboard. Filed as MORF-286, 5 points.

**One session core under the three engines.** `engine.ts`, `mw/engine.ts`, `rev/engine.ts`
share about 146 lines (DotU and MW) and 86 (Revenge) of key queue, edit-taking, save, die,
changed and finish that are not a port of anything, plus three copies of `CharacterFile`,
`KeyHandler`, the record-edited sentinel and the `Turn` record. An abstract base with
`readRecord`, `writeRecord` and `placeEdited` as hooks. Two traps: DotU's `repeatFight` must
be reset before the queue is checked or Ctrl-F stops one swing late, and Revenge must keep
filtering its clock tick out of the key log. Filed as MORF-287, 3 points.

**One copy each of the small pieces.** Five byte-identical download bodies
(`export-maps.ts:79`, `NewCharacter.svelte:244`, `SaveEditor.svelte:132`, `export-run.ts:15`,
`export-png.ts:22`). Four byte-identical `decodeDataUrl` functions (`bestiary/pictures.ts:97`,
`mw-bestiary/pictures.ts:108`, `view3d/browser.ts:48`, `mw/view3d/browser.ts:58`) and two more
base64 pairs in `storage.ts` and `run.ts`. Four `isTyping`, nine `percent`, two slugify chains.
The `.DUN` layout constants declared three times unexported (`explored.ts:31`,
`write-explored.ts:12`, `play/memory.ts:30`) and the Revenge `.BIN` constants twice. The bank's
deposit clamp and the two spell-teardown routines identical between `port/town.ts` and
`mw-port/town.ts`. Three thin components with identical markup and styles: Portrait and
MwPortrait, the three MonsterPicture canvases, Panel and MwPanel's style block, and the three
screen canvases. None of it is risky. Filed as MORF-288, 3 points.

**One monster card shell.** The list side of the bestiary is already shared. The three detail
cards (`MonsterDetail.svelte`, `MwMonsterDetail.svelte`, `RevMonsterDetail.svelte`) repeat the
wrapper, the name heading, the numbers table, the effects list and the per-level table, with
about 55 lines of CSS tripled and 29 doubled, and the styles have drifted. A `MonsterCard`
shell with art and body snippets and a plain imported stylesheet, since Svelte's scoped styles
would not reach the snippets. Also `DRAINED_STATS` twice and MW's `killExperience` re-deriving
DotU's `expValue` with the same constants. Net about 120 lines removed. Filed as MORF-289,
3 points.

**One roller session and a per-game table.** `roller/session.ts` and `mw-session.ts` are the
same class character for character apart from type names and four options; `rev-session.ts`
adds twenty lines of highlight bookkeeping. `NewCharacter.svelte` switches on the game 24
times, eight by duck-typing the view, and its sheet rows are three data tables written as
code. The component's own `GAMES` table already holds half of what those switches need. Filed
as MORF-290, 3 points.

**Town: share two helpers, not the menus.** The roller and town sweep checked the C: `bank`
(unf.c 11108, mw.c 9331) and `temple` (unf.c 10718, mw.c 9035) are the same routine three years
apart. But DotU refuses a purchase at `money < price` and MW at `price < money`, so a
character with exactly enough is turned away in MW and served in DotU; DotU's temple and store
loop and MW's are one-shot; and MW's play layer calls each building twice, once to capture the
menu and once with the answer. A shared building function would silently change one game. The
deposit clamp and the two spell-teardowns are the only identical code, and they are in
MORF-288.

**Not filed.** The display-state refactor the sweep sketched, one `Display` object with each
game's wipe rectangles as data. DotU's box comes down five different ways and MW's model is
different again. The sweep rated the risk high and there is no bug behind it. Revisit after
the tab and session shells. The `TimedScreens` generic that Revenge will need is part of
MORF-238. The MW zoom map, which today omits the door ticks, corner dots, ladder diagonals and
chute cross the game draws, is MORF-264, in progress.

## Correctness and determinism

**Revenge edits are never counted.** `src/lib/play/rev/engine.ts:304` `takeEdits()` never
calls `this.run?.edited()`; `engine.ts:578` and `mw/engine.ts:471` do. A Revenge run edited in
the save editor mid-play is logged as `edits: 0`, so `verify-run` replays it and either fails
it for a confusing reason or passes it when the edit touched nothing the replay measures. Add
the call and a Revenge case to `run.test.ts`. MORF-268.

**The site rejects its own Revenge map export.** `src/lib/play/rev/memory.ts:34` sets
`SCROLLED_ROW = 2 ** 21 - 1`, faithful to the Scroll of Seeing, which sets a 21st bit on every
row. `src/lib/map/explored.ts:319` rejects any row at or above `2 ** 20`. So
`isRevExploredFile` returns false for such a file, the Map Explorer refuses it with a wrong
size message, and `SaveEditor.svelte:98` imports it as a character. One-character fix plus a
round-trip test; the comment at `explored.ts:312` saying bit 20 is never set is true of
shipped files only. MORF-269.

**A throw inside a play loop has nowhere to go.** `run.ts:506, 538, 578` and the three Play
tabs start the loops with `void runMoveControl(session)` and the like. There is no `catch` in
any of the three engines and no `unhandledrejection` listener. In the browser the loop dies
silently and the tab freezes on its last view. Under Node the verifier crashes with a stack
trace instead of a verdict, or the replay quietly stops taking keys and the mismatch report
blames the action count. Also `rev/engine.ts:140` replaces a Revenge record that fails to parse
with an all-zero character, so a crafted `run.json` verifies against itself. And
`vite.config.ts` stamps runs with HEAD even when the tree is dirty. Catch in the loops, throw
on a bad record, append `-dirty`. MORF-270.

**Revenge's `@` and `#` keys change nothing on screen.** `rev/settings.ts:42-48` steps
`game.palette` and `game.background` as the game does, and `rev/README.md:161-163` says the
display reads them. `RevPlay.svelte:244` renders `<RevScreenCanvas screen={gameScreen} />` with
no palette, the canvas defaults to palette 0, and `rev/screen/from-game.ts` reads neither
field. `settings.test.ts` tests `revCgaPalette`, which nothing in the app calls. Pass the
palette through and read the background. MORF-271.

**Three places the Revenge port departs from the code**, found by the tidbits check and
verified in the listing (`python3 rev-tools/reference/list_basic.py ~/games/rev2/DUNSMALL.EXE
<offset> <length>`):

- `rev-bestiary/monsters.ts:137` `nameIndexOf` corrects name 20 only. The game's test at
  1000:81A6 is a range over names 19 and 20: on levels 1 to 6 they become 11 and 12, over 140
  stored hit points 21 and 22. Name 21 is reachable from 92 slots of the shipped file, and the
  bestiary lists it as never met. `MONSTERS.md:290` has the same error. MORF-272.
- `rev/monsters.ts:207` rolls a wandering monster's move against its own level plus 35. The
  game (1000:73BC) reads DGROUP B6B4, the level of the last monster met, which `clock.ts:65-69`
  already uses for the poll on purpose. B6B4 is also written on a kill at 1000:A3C8, which the
  clock's comment does not mention. MORF-273.
- `rev/ladders.ts:66` and `revmap.js:452` offer the false floor on the chute's landing level
  only. The test at 1000:0698-06BB matches the landing level or the level below it, so a chute
  allows up to two further one-level drops. Worth a DOSBox check before changing. MORF-274.

**MW's overlays use DotU's palette.** `roller/screen.ts:20` builds `SCREEN_COLOURS` from
`palettes.json`'s `m1_s1_dungeon`, and its comment says all forty palettes hold the same
sixteen fixed entries. MW's `mw-palettes.json` differs at entry 5, (53,20,10) against
(53,20,0), and entry 12, (16,0,0) against (20,0,0). `MwPlay.svelte:363` uses entry 5 as the map
box's status colour. The canvas itself is painted from `floorPalette` and is right. MORF-275.

**The roster is written on every keystroke.** `SaveEditor.svelte:220` wires `oninput` and
`onchange` to the same `edited()`, which reaches `saveRoster()` (`roster.ts:85`). That
base64-encodes every entry's bytes twice and writes the whole roster to localStorage per
input event. `storage.ts:24` swallows a quota error silently. Save on change or after a short
pause, and say so once when a write fails. MORF-276.

**Verified as fine, no action.** The key queue logs what `key()` read, not what was pressed,
and the loop is always parked at the same wait in live play and replay. The Revenge clock's
`seconds` derives from the tick count, never from the wall clock. `mulberry32` and the Borland
generator use `Math.imul` and `>>> 0` throughout; `random(n)` is integer-exact. The Revenge map
uses BigInt MBF arithmetic, never `Math.sin`. No `for…in`, no map iteration in a roll path.
Every `new DataView(x.buffer)` without an offset is on a freshly allocated array.

**Not worth an item.** `rev/treasure.ts:87-99` and `rev/town.ts:305` use fractional powers
inside `Math.floor`; V8 and Safari's libm can differ in the last bit, so a run played in Safari
could in theory fail the verifier at a rounding boundary. The odds per roll are around one in
a trillion. Note it for MORF-146 if the verifier is ever hosted off V8. `map/history.ts:44`
passes a possibly-undefined dungeon through `!` into `hasDungeon`, which checks
`Number.isInteger` and so returns false; harmless. `character/roster.ts:130` accepts any
non-empty base64 as a record and `record.ts:90` reads fixed offsets from it, so a hand-edited
storage entry could throw inside a derived; only reachable by editing localStorage by hand.
`StoredRoster` has no version field, so a renamed field would drop characters silently; add one
when the shape next changes. `play/memory.ts:393` sorts with a comparator that never returns
0, so equal slopes come out in implementation order; it feeds only the visibility set, never a
roll. Folded into MORF-281 as a while-there.

## Performance a player would feel

Measured by the performance sweep in Node with a Vite-built bench, re-checked here where cheap.

**Every keypress rebuilds the whole frame.** `Play.svelte:75` sets `view = started.view()` on
every `changed()`, and `engine.ts:644` `view()` builds fresh `place`, `box` and `monsters`
objects each time, so `Screen.svelte`'s effect reruns for every key, including a menu key or a
HIT ANY KEY that changes nothing drawn. A full DotU frame is about 10.5 ms, MW 8 ms, Revenge 4
ms, with about 4 MB of garbage per key. Half of DotU's is `view3d/frame.ts:64-75` `toRgba`,
which destructures an array per pixel: 4.9 ms against 0.9 ms for a 32-bit lookup table into a
reused buffer. Do the lookup table first, then skip the effect when nothing drawn changed.
MORF-278.

**Hidden tabs keep animating.** `App.svelte:83-122` keeps all eleven tabs mounted behind
`class:hidden`. `FloorCanvas.svelte:218-225` runs a requestAnimationFrame loop whenever the
floor has a teleporter or a you-mark, `LegendSample.svelte:33-37` runs one per animated legend
sample, and the Play tab's map loop runs once `you` is set. All of them keep drawing at 60
frames a second while another tab shows. `rev/engine.ts:224-240` `tick()` ends in
`this.changed()` unconditionally, so Revenge repaints the whole CGA screen five times a second
in the dungeon whether or not a monster moved. One `$effect` dependency on `app.tab` per loop;
`changed()` only when the poll moved something. The tick must still be logged. MORF-277.

**Slow tests.** `play/view3d/text.test.ts` takes 4 seconds alone here and about 10 in a full
run for four tests, because lines 29, 38, 48 and 59 compare 786,432-element typed arrays with
`toEqual`, which walks every element. `mw/view3d/render.test.ts:112-122` makes 307,200
`expect()` calls in a loop; `mw-bestiary/pictures.test.ts:44-47` spreads 51,200-element images
into plain arrays. `Buffer.compare` or a mismatch count. MORF-279.

**The bundle.** `dist/index.html` is 5.7 MB, 1.8 MB gzipped. The two decompiled C files are
2 MB raw and 323 KB gzipped; the 34 pictures 859 KB gzipped; both need the Source tab and
the Play tab and could only shrink with files beside the page. The realistic win inside the
single-file rule: `source/ports.ts` inlines 44 port source files as raw text while the same
code is compiled into the bundle, 148 KB gzipped, and `ui/BarChart.svelte` loads chart.js for
one bar chart, 40 KB gzipped. Together a tenth of the download. John's call on both.
MORF-280.

**Verified as fine.** Picture decoding is cached once per file in all five decoders. The Map
Explorer's hover work is a single cross-floor square lookup and one overlay draw; floor
generation runs only on floor or dungeon change. Startup parses both C files in about 6 ms.
The run log pushes one integer per key.

### Dungeons of the Unforgiven (MORF-283)

Evidence is `dotu-tools/decomp/unf.c` and `src/lib/game/port/`.

Wrong:

- "Fast Move and Invisibility are the same trick" (line 184), "the same roll", "does not
  stack". Two separate one-in-four rolls: unf.c 14206 tests DS:c058, 14237 tests DS:c059, each
  with its own `rand()`; `moment.ts:123, 146` match. Both up: a moment lost 7 in 16. Fast Move's
  roll comes before the poison and disease tick, so it pauses the ailment clock a quarter of
  the time. Invisibility also wins the first move on the one engagement in three that would
  lose it: unf.c 14856 `if (DAT_6000_c059 == '\0') goto`, `combat.ts` attackTiming.
- "Youth costs you a tenth of everything" (193), "the level you drop back to has to be earned
  again". No level is dropped. `magic.ts:678` writes `exp = 0.9 * exp` and nothing else;
  `gain_level` is only called from `flea_inn` inside `check_gain_level` (unf.c 11090-11092).
- "The snake calls you a wimp every time you start the game" (706), the deepest floor "never
  written to or read from the save". `save_player` writes the whole record, unf.c 12609
  `fwrite(&DAT_6000_b880, 0xa87, ...)`, and DS:c179 is inside it at offset 0x8f9;
  `record.ts:164` reads it. Only a new character starts at zero. `hints.ts:262` and
  `dotu-tools/docs/TIDBITS.md` repeat the error.
- "Every trap door on a floor drops you on the same square" (439), "on most floors it is
  column 18, row 93". Re-run here over all 325 floors with `unfmap.js` trapdoorDest: (17,93)
  on 110 floors, (18,93) on 86, (18,92) on 58, (16,94) on 27. Per module (17,93) wins in II,
  III and V, (18,93) in IV, I ties 8 to 8. `formulas.ts:163` has the same sentence.
- "The walls wear the last monster's colour" (760). The wall-face drawer sets the tint itself
  before each face: unf.c 19350 `_DAT_6000_4fbd = 0xc` for a plain face, 1, 0 or 15 for the
  type-4 face at 19337-19344, gated on DS:2306 and DS:031f being zero. PICTURES.md section 4
  says the same.
- "Module II's town is Module I's town with the buildings moved" (457), towns III and V have
  "not one wall in common". Re-run here: none of the blocks draws the same pattern, but
  thousands of wall sides are walls in both and about 5,400 of 8,800 squares agree on rock or
  open. Say "no block draws the same wall pattern". The sweep's count of ten differing
  building squares I did not re-run.
- "Seventeen floors are dealt the same walls twice" (482), "three of them in three modules at
  once". `twins.ts` has seventeen groups over fifteen floor numbers, since 8 and 22 each
  appear twice, and two groups span three modules: floor 3 in I, II, V and floor 13 in II,
  IV, V. The page's own list shows exactly those two.
- "The Shadow bosses are holes in the shape of another monster" (389), "the only thing that
  differs between the two records is the tint colour". `dotu-data.json`: Shadow Gargalon is
  special 100, type 5, expMult 16, colour 32; Gargalon is 0, 6, 1, 52. Only the picture
  differs by tint alone.

Misleading:

- "The easy setting's spell point bonus never happens" (675). The 1.5 times is right, but
  the setting also buys `strike` adding strength to the to-hit total a second time and 25
  more over STR 25 (`combat.ts:101`, 2000:7e36), `random(7000)` extra dollars on every money
  drop (`drops.ts:524`), crystals at a third off (`town.ts:145`), and loses Module V
  (`modules.ts:44`).
- "Ascend gives up two floors before it says it does" (214). The test is `level > 65`
  (`magic.ts:892, 956, 1065`) and the message says 64'TH (`:830`), so the spell works two
  floors deeper than it admits. Retitle.
- "The damage cap is a cliff, not a ceiling" (127). The cap at `combat.ts:433` sits inside
  `if (damage > 0 && pc.level > pc.lev)` at :422, so it only applies while the floor is
  deeper than the character's level, and the level-0 cap, the under-3 halving and the breath
  swap come after it.
- "Every monster you kill becomes the same garbage can" (321), the drops "scale with the
  floor". `kills.ts:286-291`: weapon and armour roll against the slot's level, already zeroed,
  so only money and the you-find drop scale.
- "The permanent enchantments set the plus, they do not add to it" (236), "added on top of the
  item's when you swing or are hit". `defend` subtracts `tempArmorPlus` (`combat.ts:404`) and
  never the armour's own plus, which line 628's tidbit says. Armour's counts on its own.
- "There is no map, anywhere" (423), "sixteen squares by sixteen", "about a third". The 25
  patterns are 32 by 32: `unfmap.js:62-63` indexes `pattern*0x200 + ((x>>4)&1)*0x100 +
  ((y>>4)&1)*0x80`, so a block takes the 16 by 16 quarter its parity picks. The open share is
  35 to 43 percent.
- "Nobody starts with anything in the bank" (535), the bank "a little further along". Bank is
  0x458 and crystals 0x46c (`state.ts:134, 136`), so earlier.
- "Five messages nobody can be shown" (808), "notes that appear under a monster". FUN_3000_6a6a
  (unf.c 20667) reads a UH2.BIN tablet and prints it in the message-box column; it is
  triggered from the random-events tick for a character under level 3.
- "Drain Monster kills pay nothing" (202). A level-0 kill pays `expMult * 6`
  (`dotu-mech.js` expValue), 6 to 30 for the regulars. Retitle.

Unverified, left as is: "A message hidden from the hex editor" (743), whether it is the only

### Moraff's World (MORF-284)

Evidence is `mw-tools/decomp/mw.c` and `src/lib/game/mw-port/`. Class numbers: 0 fighter,
1 worshipper, 2 monk, 3 wizard, 4 priest, 5 sage, 6 mage.

Wrong:

- "Changing dungeon does not clear the map you have explored" (562), "the files on disk are
  left where they are". Leaving the world map into a different dungeon number calls
  FUN_2000_70ef (mw.c 21774-21775), which is eight `unlink`s of the slot's ?0.DUN to ?7.DUN
  (11623-11651). The map starts blank; only the same number keeps it.
- "Swing on the beat" (124), the monster's roll "with the tick count plus 100". `monster_turn`
  does `srand(ticks + 100)` at 11178 but rolls with `random_n` at 11180, which reseeds itself
  first from a running sum of clock readings (8816-8819). Only the player's swing is on the
  sawtooth.
- "Almost every random number is a reading of the clock" (892). Same +100 sentence, and the
  stocking reseeds `srand` from the clock plus a counter before each placement (10089-10101)
  rather than using `random_n`.
- "Ascend and Descend put you anywhere on the floor" (344). Five floor spells, not six.
  Major Descend adds 25 and caps at floor 75: 16288-16291 `+ 0x19` then `0x4b`. Neither
  Descend has the Ascends' floor-1 refusal (16135, 16211, 16332), so "only castable between
  floors 1 and 65" is wrong; `effects.ts:193` and `magic.ts:1025` repeat it.
- "Constitution is the only thing that takes a deep floor back" (156). The multiply
  (11264-11268) is inside the same `0 < damage && level < floor` block as the extra rolls
  (11232-11272), so a character whose level has caught the floor gets no reduction at all.
- "A level 0 character cannot be hit for more than five" (192). Four: 11273-11279 is
  `rand*4/0x8000 + 1`. And the breath branch (11518-11519) comes after and is uncapped.
- "Fast Move and Invisibility stack" (318). Inverted arithmetic. Two rolls (12299-12307,
  12330-12338) each skip the pass 1 in 4, so both up skips 7 in 16 and runs 9 in 16.
- "Youth halves your age" (329), "a floor at about thirty days". 16004-16008: 0x3d80 =
  15,744 minutes, about eleven days at the page's own 525,600 minutes a year. `effects.ts:108`
  already says 15,744.
- "A monk starts with every spell in the game" (70), "most classes one spell, the wizardly
  and priestly classes two". 20287-20295: Little Cure to everyone but the fighter, Zap to
  classes 3, 5, 6, Strength to 1, 4, 5. So fighter 0; worshipper, wizard, priest, mage 2;
  sage 3; monk everything.
- "The floor slosher never runs out" (111), "the nearest one that is not rock", "five of them
  are used up". 24920-24926 rerolls a random square until it is not rock. Of the six kill
  finds (24199-24266) four are spent; the ring of regeneration is passive.
- "Insert the game disk" (851), "watching for a drive change". 9701-9718 polls kbhit/getch
  and the mouse button; Escape or the right button quits. Nothing looks at the drive.
- "The walls change colour every eleven floors" (865), pictures "only ever use pixel values 1
  to 18". Decoded here with `dotu-pic.js` on `~/games/mworld/WORLD.PIC`: one image, the Stone
  Giant, has a handful of value-24 pixels, a wall-set entry.

Misleading:

- "A priest can write wizard scrolls" (22), "anyone can cast". A fighter cannot read a scroll
  or use a wand at all: 16927-16936 refuses class 0 for types 1 to 3.
- "A secret door is a wall on the map and nothing else" (549), a door "a line with a gap". The
  full line is drawn for any value but 3, then two short bars across it for a door

- "One monster attack in four is thrown away" (171), "after all of that arithmetic". The roll
  (11218-11231) comes after the dice but before the deep-floor extras and the constitution
  divide.
- "A big swing rolls the damage die several times" (142), "every full 40 points". The loop is
  `0x28 < total` (10937), so 41 already rolls once.
- "Three of the fourteen lessons say nothing at all" (753), "print an empty box". Cases 0xb
  to 0xd are bare breaks (22011-22016); nothing is drawn.
- "Seven kinds of money" (614), "just under four jewels". Divisors 200, 12, 4, 2 (9354-9363)
  give at most 199/200 + 11/12 + 3/4 + 1/2, a little over three.
- "Sleep is the one battle spell the ten cannot refuse" (83). `spell_proof`'s callers are
  teleport_monster, autokill, drain_monster and spell_effect's two Hold Monsters (15489), so

- "Everything more than a few steps away is standing still" (466), "walking distance". The

### Moraff's Revenge (MORF-285)

Evidence is the listing from `rev-tools/reference/list_basic.py` over
`~/games/rev2/DUNSMALL.EXE`, read-only, and the docs under `rev-tools/docs`. Offsets are
code-segment offsets. Several errors were inherited from the docs, which should be corrected
with the page.

Wrong:

- "A monster comes through the wall at you" (343), "the only thing that refuses a step is
  another monster", "a 2 where the generation goes". 758D-75CC is `(level + 2) / B488 * kind *
  column * row + 10`, SIN, times 10, ABS, INT, compared with 7. B488 is the character's own
  generation (DUNGEON.md:224). The 2 is on the level. A monster is stopped by a wall and walks
  through a door as the character does; `monsters.ts` blocked already does that.
- "One of the twenty-two names is never met" (374). 81A6-81C5 is a range test over 19 and 20;
  81D8 adds -8 on levels under 7, 820F adds 2 when the stored hit points pass 0x8c. Name 21 is
  reachable; 92 slots of the shipped 2.NUM reach it. MONSTERS.md:290 and the port share the
  error. MORF-272.
- "A building is up a rope, and the game's own map never shows one" (487). 53CF and 47A0 both
  call C102, which puts a letter sprite on all ten building squares; MAP-MEMORY.md:122 and
  DUNGEON.md:519 say so. Only the feature branch is gated on 7.NUM.
- "A characteristic is stored three times as big" (116), "never appear on the game's
  statistics screen", "the top of the scale". 1A9A-1ADD is `FOR I = 1 TO 6: PRINT USING` with
  the format strings at 0x2126 and the characteristics at 0x1f92. And 22 is the highest any
  shipped character has, not a ceiling; the roll has no cap. SURVEY.md:510 has the error.
- "The deeper it lives, the harder it hunts" (388), "its own level plus 35", "its level". Both
  73BC and 7EEC read B6B4, the last monster met's level, written at 80EF on meeting and A3C8
  on a kill. The port's wander roll shares the slip. MORF-273.
- "No monster ever stands on the outer ring" (442). Spawns avoid the border, but a step clamps
  to 1..20 and 1..19 (75F0, 7613, 7636, 765C) and is written back at 76EE, so monsters walk
  onto it.
- "The wizard's guild charges the level to the power 1.75" (545), "it sells one level of
  spells". 2DED-2EE7 prints PREP SPELLS or BATTLE SPELLS with the two texts and subtracts the
  price from B588; nothing marks a spell known. It sells the description.
- "A chute drops one, two or three levels, and the false floor is the same chute again" (320).
  064D sets the square's code to 1 and D adds it to the level (0E0F-0E19): a fixed one-level
  drop. And 0698-06BB matches the landing level or the level below it, so a chute allows two
  further drops. DUNGEON.md section 8 and the port have the landing only. MORF-274.

Misleading:

- "Nothing waits for you until a monster is beside you" (71), "the moment one is on your
  square that changes". The fight prompt still polls: 86E2 calls 7EEC before its INKEY$. Only
  the wand and item sub-prompts block.
- "Three inns, and the cheap ones can rob you" (502). 1EE2 zeroes the money and also the
  knife, sword, mace and both weapon pluses (1F0C-1F2A; `town.ts:48-59`).
- "Which dungeon you walk in is a field of your character" (167), the map "describes somewhere
  that no longer exists". The fountain zeroes rows 1 to 20 of levels 1 to 70 first
  (3DCC-3E2E); there is no stale map.
- "What is on a square is a second formula" (289), "1 to 9 a ladder, 0 a chute, 50 nothing". 50
  is what the caller writes when the 7.NUM bit is clear (5500), never a formula result; a code
  of 10 or more can still yield a ladder down when a lower level folds to the distance (552B).

Right on the page, wrong in the port: "Seven of the town's ladders lead to floor minus one"
(575). The game adds the negative code with no clamp (0E38-0E45); `rev/engine.ts:282` clamps
at 0 and the README's departures list does not say so. Add it to the list, or check in
DOSBox what floor -1 does. In MORF-285's notes.

Unverified, left as is: "seventeen of them are yours" (85), no comparison against 17 exists in
the module, only NCD.EXE's order text; "two names in F5.COM" (676), the file is dated 2026;
"A zero in your saved map still carries the bytes" (640), the leftover bytes are the same in a
2026-written file, so they are CHCHAR's arithmetic, not stale memory, and the BRUN30 claim is
asserted rather than read. Duplicates: 554 and 575; 98 and 116; 57 and 388.
